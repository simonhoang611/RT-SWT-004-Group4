\subsection{Dataset Construction}

The dataset pairs 261 project-maintained Gherkin files with Connextra-style stories reverse-engineered from those files by GPT-5.4-mini at temperature 0. The prompt received at most the first 15,000 characters; this truncated 84 Fineract files. Generated files were scored against the full stored reference. \subsection{Model, Prompts and Recording}

All requests specified \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17}~\cite{openai_gpt54mini} through the Chat Completions API~\cite{openai_chat_api}, with temperature 0 and no output-token cap. The returned model name was recorded for C1, C2 and the rerun, but not original C0. C0 uses one same-project exemplar (ids 2, 102, 202); C1 removes the exemplar; C2 uses different same-project exemplars (ids 86, 119, 203). The primary C0 analysis excludes its three exemplars ($N=258$); paired ablation uses 255 files that are non-exemplars in every condition. Full system messages, exemplar pairs, mapping and SHA-256 manifest are in Supplementary Section~S1 (Table~S1; Listings~S1--S2) and the versioned replication package.

C0, C1 and C2 were rerun on 28 October 2026 with one script and logging path; four additional runs per condition used a fixed, project-stratified subset of 60 files. The same-time rerun also tested C0 with the proposal's 1,500-token cap (C0cap). The analysis plan and its hash were fixed before the rerun. C0 remains the primary result; a paired median change smaller than within-file run variation is not interpreted as an exemplar effect.

\textbf{Failure policy.} C0 used a 600-s client timeout and retried errors, including rate limits, after 60 s without a retry limit. C1/C2 allowed five attempts with waits of 5, 10, 20, 40 and 60 s; the rerun used a 600-s timeout, disabled client retries and allowed five attempts with 5, 10, 20 and 40-s waits. Empty outputs or terminal failures would count as parser FAILs and remain in the denominator, while empty outputs would be excluded from cosine analysis. All 261 C0 outputs and all C1/C2 and rerun requests succeeded on the first attempt; none was empty. Supplementary Section~S3 records the run-specific policy and logs.

\subsection{Measures and Defect Audit}

\textbf{Skeleton cosine.} We retain Gherkin keyword lines, replace quoted strings and numbers with \texttt{<PARAM>} and \texttt{<NUM>}, and drop tags, tables, comments and other lines (Table~S2). The skeletons are encoded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2~\cite{reimers2019,minilm_card}, mean-pooled and truncated at 256 word-piece tokens. The score is cosine similarity between generated and reference embeddings:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SkelCos}(g,r)=\frac{g\cdot r}{\lVert g\rVert\lVert r\rVert}.
\end{equation}
Masking makes the score blind to values; truncation affects 67.4\% of reference and 64.3\% of generated skeletons. It measures structural resemblance, not business correctness. The preregistered 0.85 is an uncalibrated descriptive reference point, not an acceptance criterion; sensitivity is reported from 0.80 to 0.90 (Table~S3).

\textbf{Parser compliance.} ESR is the share of generated files accepted by the \texttt{behave} 1.3.3 parser~\cite{behave133} without step definitions. A pass confirms valid Gherkin syntax, not step matching, execution or behavioral correctness. The 80\% value is the prespecified RQ2 comparison rate.

\textbf{Defect audit.} We sampled 60 files with cosine $\geq0.85$ (20 per project) and labelled business-logic and temporal/causal defects against each story's criteria. OpenAI Codex drafted the labels; a second author reviewed them in one confirmation covering all 60 files. A first author independently labelled 15 files (6 Sylius, 3 Fineract, 6 Diaspora); agreement with the reviewed labels was Cohen's $\kappa=0.571$ and $0.609$ for the two facets~\cite{cohen1960}, below the protocol's 0.7 target. The raters then held a per-file consensus round on those 15; the other 45 were not relabelled. The audit is exploratory. A separate keyword-order check and an eight-operator perturbation test on 120 scenarios (623 valid pairs) are detailed in Supplementary Section~S2.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

Confirmatory tests use $\alpha=0.05$, following guidance on statistical testing in software engineering~\cite{arcuri2011}. For RQ1, the exact one-sided sign test tests whether the median exceeds 0.85; the median has a 95\% percentile-bootstrap CI (10,000 resamples, seed 2026)~\cite{efron1993}, and the preregistered Wilcoxon test is a sensitivity analysis because the differences are skewed. We report $\delta=P(x>0.85)-P(x<0.85)$ with a CI. For RQ2, an exact binomial test~\cite{agresti2002} compares parser compliance with 80\%; we report a Clopper--Pearson CI~\cite{clopper1934} and risk difference. Exploratory project comparisons use Kruskal--Wallis~\cite{kruskal1952} and Holm-adjusted Mann--Whitney tests~\cite{holm1979}; effect sizes are reported with CIs and no categorical magnitude labels. Leakage uses Spearman's $\rho$. Further test details are in Supplementary Section~S3.
