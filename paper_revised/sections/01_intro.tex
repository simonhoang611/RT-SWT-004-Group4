\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Behavior-driven development expresses expected software behavior in executable or executable-adjacent scenarios, commonly using Gherkin. In practice, the cost of BDD is not limited to writing Given--When--Then syntax. Engineers must preserve domain preconditions, project conventions, data dependencies, and expected outcomes while keeping feature files parsable by the project toolchain. A generated scenario can therefore be syntactically valid yet incomplete as a specification of business behavior.

The three projects in this study illustrate this distinction. A Sylius checkout scenario may depend on channel, country, shipping-method state, and confirmation behavior. Apache Fineract scenarios frequently encode dates, repayment periods, balances, and data tables. Diaspora scenarios emphasize browser-visible behavior such as navigation, tags, comments, and notifications. Converting a compact Connextra story into project-consistent Gherkin is consequently a constrained generation problem rather than a simple paraphrasing task.

\subsection{Research Gap and Motivation}

Prior work shows that LLMs can generate BDD artifacts, but existing evaluations differ substantially in data scale, model family, and validation method. Rathnayake et al.\ evaluated GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini on 500 user stories from four proprietary software products and reported a BERTScore F1 of 91.16\% for \mbox{GPT-4}~\cite{rathnayake2026}. Karpurapu et al.\ reported fewer Gherkin-lint syntax errors under few-shot prompting than zero-shot prompting; across their evaluated models, zero-shot outputs accounted for 89\% of the observed syntax errors~\cite{karpurapu2024}. Ferreira et al.\ evaluated a GPT-4 Turbo industrial acceptance-testing pipeline that generated Gherkin scenarios and downstream Cypress tests~\cite{ferreira2025}, while Fernandes et al.\ compared several LLMs on a much smaller sample from a single production system~\cite{fernandes2025}. These studies establish feasibility, but they do not jointly answer whether a current compact model can produce both parser-ready and structurally close Gherkin across multiple public software domains. In particular, none of the five studies in Table~\ref{tab:delta} tests whether its semantic measure reacts to business-logic defects, and none varies the example given in the prompt.

This study addresses that narrower gap by evaluating the pinned gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17 snapshot on 261 stories from three unrelated open-source projects, using same-project one-shot prompting and two separate outcome measures. The emphasis is not that GPT-5.4-mini is inherently superior to earlier models. Rather, the contribution is an auditable evaluation design that separates syntactic validity from structural similarity on a public, multi-domain benchmark, and that tests how far the similarity metric can be trusted. Because the stories were reverse-engineered from the reference files and the 0.85 reference point was not calibrated, this revised version adds a leakage measurement, a defect audit of high-scoring outputs, a perturbation test of the metric, a sensitivity analysis over reference points, per-project results, a prompt ablation, a same-time rerun with repeated runs and a consensus round on the doubly labelled files.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}

\textbf{RQ1 -- Structural skeleton resemblance.} When GPT-5.4-mini reconstructs feature files from user stories reverse-engineered from them, how is the skeleton cosine similarity to the reference distributed around the reference point of 0.85 fixed in the proposal?

\noindent\textbf{RQ2 -- Syntactic validity.} Does GPT-5.4-mini generate feature files that the behave Gherkin parser accepts at a rate above 80\%?

The paper makes three contributions: (1) a 261-case, multi-domain benchmark derived from project-maintained reference files in Sylius, Apache Fineract, and Diaspora, with its construction and leakage risk documented; (2) an evaluation that reports parser compliance separately from structural similarity and checks the similarity metric against defect labels, seeded defects and alternative reference points; and (3) a replication package containing the pinned model identifier, prompts, scripts, API logs, outputs, and all revision analyses.
