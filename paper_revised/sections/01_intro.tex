\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Behavior-driven development expresses expected software behavior in executable or executable-adjacent scenarios, commonly using Gherkin. In practice, the cost of BDD is not limited to writing Given--When--Then syntax. Engineers must preserve domain preconditions, project conventions, data dependencies, and expected outcomes while keeping feature files parsable by the project toolchain. A generated scenario can therefore be syntactically valid yet incomplete as a specification of business behavior.

The three projects in this study illustrate this distinction. A Sylius checkout scenario may depend on channel, country, shipping-method state, and confirmation behavior. Apache Fineract scenarios frequently encode dates, repayment periods, balances, and data tables. Diaspora scenarios emphasize browser-visible behavior such as navigation, tags, comments, and notifications. Converting a compact Connextra story into project-consistent Gherkin is consequently a constrained generation problem rather than a simple paraphrasing task.

\subsection{Research Gap and Motivation}

Prior work shows that LLMs can generate BDD artifacts, but existing evaluations differ substantially in data scale, model family, and validation method. Rathnayake et al.\ evaluated GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini on a proprietary BDD dataset and reported a BERTScore F1 of 91.16\% for GPT-4~\cite{rathnayake2026}. Karpurapu et al.\ reported fewer Gherkin-lint syntax errors under few-shot prompting than zero-shot prompting; across their evaluated models, zero-shot outputs accounted for 89\% of the observed syntax errors~\cite{karpurapu2024}. Ferreira et al.\ evaluated a GPT-4 Turbo industrial acceptance-testing pipeline that generated Gherkin scenarios and downstream Cypress tests~\cite{ferreira2025}, while Fernandes et al.\ compared several LLMs on a much smaller public sample~\cite{fernandes2025}. These studies establish feasibility, but they do not jointly answer whether a current compact model can produce both parser-ready and semantically close Gherkin across multiple public software domains.

This study addresses that narrower gap by evaluating the pinned gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17 snapshot on 261 stories from three unrelated open-source projects, using same-project one-shot prompting and two separate outcome measures. The emphasis is not that GPT-5.4-mini is inherently superior to earlier models. Rather, the contribution is an auditable evaluation design that separates syntactic validity from semantic similarity on a public, multi-domain benchmark, and that tests how far the similarity metric can be trusted. Because the stories were reverse-engineered from the reference files and the threshold was not calibrated, this revised version adds a leakage measurement, a defect audit of high-scoring outputs, a perturbation test of the metric, a threshold sensitivity analysis, per-project results and a prompt ablation.

\subsection{Research Questions and Contributions}

\textbf{RQ1 -- Skeleton similarity.} Does the median skeleton cosine similarity between GPT-5.4-mini's generated files and their references exceed the proposal's threshold of 0.85?

\noindent\textbf{RQ2 -- Syntactic validity.} Does GPT-5.4-mini generate feature files that the behave Gherkin parser accepts at a rate above 80\%?

The paper makes three contributions: (1) a 261-case, multi-domain benchmark derived from expert-written Gherkin in Sylius, Apache Fineract, and Diaspora, with its construction and leakage risk documented; (2) an evaluation that reports parser compliance separately from semantic similarity and checks the similarity metric against defect labels, seeded defects and alternative thresholds; and (3) a replication package with the pinned model snapshot, prompts, scripts, API logs, outputs and all revision analyses.
