\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: Structural Skeleton Resemblance}

Among 258 non-exemplar files, the median cosine is 0.853 (95\% bootstrap CI 0.844--0.859); 136 are above and 122 below 0.85. The exact one-sided sign test gives $p=0.209$ and $\delta=0.054$ (95\% CI $-0.072$ to 0.179; Table~S6 and Fig.~S1). Differences are left-skewed (skewness $-0.41$, 95\% CI $-0.76$ to $-0.08$; mean 0.848), so the Wilcoxon sensitivity test concerns the pseudo-median (Hodges--Lehmann estimate 0.850; $p=0.474$). Including the three exemplars gives sign-test $p=0.161$ and Wilcoxon $p=0.355$. We fail to reject $H_0$ in all four analyses.

\subsection{Results per Project}
\label{sec:res-h1}

Sylius is the only project with a median above 0.85 (0.887); Fineract and Diaspora medians are 0.826 and 0.831 (Table~\ref{tab:domain}; Fig.~S2). Project medians differ (Kruskal--Wallis $H=60.87$, $p<0.001$, $\eta^2_H=0.231$, 95\% CI 0.144--0.336); Holm-adjusted tests distinguish Sylius from both other projects ($p<0.001$), but not Fineract from Diaspora ($p=0.74$).

\textbf{Truncated story input.} The 84 Fineract files whose stories used only the first 15,000 characters do not score lower than the 15 non-truncated files (median 0.827 vs. 0.824; Mann--Whitney $p=0.99$). Prefix rescoring changes one score by $-0.004$; the Fineract and pooled medians remain 0.826 and 0.853 (details in Supplementary Section~S3).

\subsection{Sensitivity to the Reference Point}
\label{sec:res-b3}

The pooled median is significantly above candidate reference points through 0.84 (at 0.84, sign-test $p=0.002$, Wilcoxon $p=0.0097$), but not at 0.85 or higher (Table~S3). Project rankings remain stable. The audit cannot calibrate the reference point: it samples only files at or above 0.85, and cosine does not distinguish defective from non-defective files within that range (Mann--Whitney $p=0.21$).

\subsection{Defects in High-Similarity Outputs}
\label{sec:res-defects}

\textbf{Exploratory audit.} Of 60 files with cosine $\geq0.85$, 25 (41.7\%, 95\% CI [29.1, 55.1]) have a severity-2-or-3 defect under the reviewed labels: 6/20 Sylius, 16/20 Fineract and 3/20 Diaspora. Both facets flag the same files. The most frequent defects are wrong expected outcomes (20 files) and outcomes without a verifying Then (19). This project-balanced high-similarity sample does not estimate the corpus defect rate; Supplementary Section~S3 gives an example.

\textbf{Agreement and consensus.} On 15 doubly labelled files, the independent rater agreed on 12 per facet; $\kappa=0.571$ (95\% CI 0.05--1.00) for business logic and 0.609 (0.24--1.00) for temporal/causal defects. After consensus, 6 files have business-logic and 8 temporal/causal defects, compared with 5 and 5 under reviewed labels. The other 45 files were not relabelled; applying consensus labels only to the 15 would yield 28/60 defective files (26 business-logic).

The keyword-order check flags 5 of 255 parseable generated files and 40 of 244 parseable references. In 623 valid perturbation pairs, no seeded defect lowers cosine below 0.8995; this scenario-level test does not estimate a whole-file false-negative rate (Section~S2).

\subsection{Lexical Leakage from Reverse Engineering}
\label{sec:res-b1}

Median story--reference trigram overlap is 4.9\% (Sylius 8.3\%, Fineract 1.6\%, Diaspora 5.8\%). Overlap correlates with cosine in Sylius ($\rho=0.29$, 95\% CI 0.11--0.46, $p=0.003$) and Diaspora (0.33, 0.08--0.55, $p=0.009$), but not Fineract (0.11, $-0.11$--0.31, $p=0.30$). The pooled $\rho=0.43$ (95\% CI 0.33--0.53) is inflated by project differences.

\subsection{RQ2: Parser Compliance}

Of 258 non-exemplar files, 255 pass (98.84\%, 95\% CI [96.64\%, 99.76\%], exact binomial $p<0.001$; Table~S4). The risk difference from 80\% is $+18.84$ percentage points (95\% CI [16.64, 19.76]). We reject $H_0$.

\subsection{Prompt Ablation}
\label{sec:res-h2}

The same-time rerun compares 255 common files (Table~\ref{tab:h2}). Removing the exemplar (C1) lowers cosine in 193 files; the median paired difference is $-0.030$ (95\% CI $-0.038$ to $-0.021$, Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$), exceeding within-file SD. Changing the exemplar (C2) raises cosine in 140 files and lowers it in 115. The median paired difference is $+0.008$ (95\% CI $-0.001$ to 0.015); the paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives a Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$. This shift is below the within-file SD and is not interpreted as a robust exemplar effect. The mean C2--C0 difference is $+0.004$ (95\% CI $-0.011$ to 0.020), with per-file intervals on both sides of zero (Section~\ref{sec:res-rep}). Parser counts are 255, 252 and 254; Holm-adjusted McNemar $p=0.50$ and 1.0. Table~S7 reports the original runs.

\subsection{Run-to-Run Variation}
\label{sec:res-rep}

Across 60 repeated files, ICC(1,1) is 0.921, 0.904 and 0.929 for C0/C1/C2; within-file SD is 0.020, 0.022 and 0.019. Parser outcomes changed in 0/2/2 files, while 19/13/14 files crossed 0.85 across runs. Between original and rerun C0, 45 files crossed the reference point, but the new median is 0.852 (sign-test $p=0.247$) and all 258 parse. The per-file Welch CI for C2--C0 lies wholly above zero for 19 files, wholly below for 17 and includes zero for 24. Changing the exemplar moves individual files both ways, with no detectable average effect.
