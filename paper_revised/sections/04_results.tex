\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: Skeleton Similarity}

RQ1 asks whether the median skeleton cosine similarity of C0 exceeds 0.85. On the 258 non-exemplar files the median is 0.853, 0.003 above the threshold, and the one-sided Wilcoxon test gives $p = 0.474$ with $\delta = 0.054$ (Table~\ref{tab:rq1}; Fig.~\ref{fig:dist} in Appendix~\ref{app:details}). Including the three exemplars gives $p = 0.355$. Decision: fail to reject $H_0$ in both analyses.

\subsection{Results per Project}
\label{sec:res-h1}

The pooled median hides large differences between projects (Table~\ref{tab:domain}; Fig.~\ref{fig:domain} in Appendix~\ref{app:details}). Sylius is the only project above the threshold: its median is 0.887 and 77.8\% of its files score at least 0.85. Fineract (median 0.826) and Diaspora (median 0.831) are significantly below 0.85 (one-sided $p < 0.001$ and $p = 0.008$). The projects differ (Kruskal--Wallis $H = 60.87$, df $= 2$, $p < 0.001$, $\eta^2_H = 0.231$). Sylius differs from Fineract and from Diaspora (Holm-adjusted $p < 0.001$ for both), while the difference between Fineract and Diaspora is not significant ($p = 0.74$). Spread is also uneven: the interquartile range is largest for Diaspora (0.087) and smallest for Fineract (0.062). The pooled result therefore does not describe a general capability: it averages one project above the threshold with two below it.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the Threshold}
\label{sec:res-b3}

Because 0.85 was not calibrated, we repeated the RQ1 test for thresholds between 0.80 and 0.90 (Table~\ref{tab:sweep} in Appendix~\ref{app:details}). The pooled median lies significantly above every threshold up to 0.84 ($p = 0.0097$ at 0.84) and above none from 0.85 upwards. The conclusion of RQ1 therefore depends on where the threshold is placed within a few hundredths. The project ranking does not: at every threshold Sylius has the largest share of files above it, and Fineract the smallest from 0.83 upwards.

We could not calibrate the threshold against defect labels. The defect audit (Section~\ref{sec:res-defects}) sampled only files at or above 0.85, so it cannot estimate how often files below a threshold are defective. Within the audited range, cosine did not differ significantly between defective and non-defective files (Mann--Whitney $p = 0.21$; AUC 0.40 when a higher cosine is read as predicting a defect, i.e.\ defective files tended to score slightly lower). With 60 files from a restricted range, this neither shows nor rules out that the metric discriminates. Defects occurred in 11 of 24 files between 0.85 and 0.875, 10 of 16 between 0.875 and 0.90, 2 of 11 between 0.90 and 0.95, and 2 of 9 at 0.95 or above.

\subsection{Defects in High-Similarity Outputs}
\label{sec:res-defects}

\textbf{Defect audit.} Of the 60 audited files, all with skeleton cosine of at least 0.85, 25 (41.7\%, 95\% CI [29.1, 55.1]) contain at least one defect of severity 2 or 3: 6 of 20 Sylius files, 16 of 20 Fineract files and 3 of 20 Diaspora files. The two facets flag exactly the same 25 files, so we report them as one result, not as two independent findings. The most frequent business-logic defects are wrong expected outcomes (20 files), missing or wrong conditions (9) and wrong numbers (8); no wrong operator or lost negation was found. On the temporal/causal facet, outcomes without a verifying Then are most frequent (19 files), followed by Then steps that do not follow from the When (9) and missing preconditions (8). The share of 41.7\% describes a sample balanced across projects and restricted to high-scoring files; it is not an estimate of the defect rate of the whole corpus.

For example, a criterion in the story of Sylius file 76 expects a cart total of \$73.80 after ``one taxed product'' is removed from a cart holding three units of a \$100 product and two units of a \$30 product, both taxed at 23\%. That total corresponds to removing the \$100 product entirely. The generated scenario removes a single unit and still expects \$73.80, although its own data imply \$319.80. The file scores 0.914, and both the reviewed labels and the independent rater mark it as defective.

\textbf{Agreement.} On the 15 doubly labelled files, the independent rater agreed with the reviewed labels on 12 files for each facet. Cohen's $\kappa$ was 0.571 for the business-logic facet and 0.609 for the temporal/causal facet. Both values are below the threshold of 0.7 set in our protocol. Of the six disagreements across the two facets, five concern three Diaspora files that the independent rater marked as defective and the reviewed labels did not; the sixth is a Sylius file in the opposite direction. All three Fineract files received the same labels.

\textbf{Structural checks.} The keyword-order check flags 5 of the 255 parseable generated files and 40 of the 244 parseable reference files. Generated files thus follow the Given--When--Then order more consistently than the references. The audit shows, however, that correct order does not imply correct content.

\textbf{Perturbation test.} In the 623 valid scenario pairs, no seeded defect lowered skeleton cosine below 0.8995. Changed numbers and quoted values leave the skeleton identical by construction, and swapped or reordered steps score at least 0.954, although the keyword-order check flags all of them (Appendix~\ref{app:perturb}). These results show insensitivity at the scenario level and do not estimate a false-negative rate for whole files.

\subsection{Lexical Leakage from Reverse Engineering}
\label{sec:res-b1}

The user stories are short compared with the references: a median of 4.9\% of the distinct word trigrams of a reference file also occur in its story (Sylius 8.3\%, Fineract 1.6\%, Diaspora 5.8\%). Higher overlap goes with higher similarity in two of the three projects: Spearman's $\rho$ between overlap and skeleton cosine is 0.29 for Sylius ($p = 0.003$), 0.33 for Diaspora ($p = 0.009$) and 0.11 for Fineract ($p = 0.30$). The pooled value ($\rho = 0.43$) is inflated by the project effect, since Sylius is high on both measures. These correlations are consistent with part of the similarity coming from wording that the story carries over from the reference, but they do not establish a causal effect. No independently written backlog was available to test this directly.

\subsection{RQ2: Parser Compliance}

RQ2 asks whether more than 80\% of the generated files are valid Gherkin. Of the 258 non-exemplar files, 255 pass the parser (98.84\%, 95\% CI [96.64\%, 99.76\%], $p < 0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:rq2} in Appendix~\ref{app:details}). The three failures (files 136, 148 and 154) are an Examples block outside a Scenario Outline in two files and a malformed data table in one. Decision: reject $H_0$. This corrects the 100\% reported in the submitted version, which came from the faulty dry-run check described in Section~\ref{sec:method}; the decision is unchanged.

\subsection{Prompt Ablation}
\label{sec:res-h2}

All 261 requests of C1 and of C2 returned an output (Table~\ref{tab:h2}; 255 common files). Removing the example (C1) lowered skeleton cosine in 182 of the 255 files (median paired difference $-0.028$; paired Wilcoxon, Holm-adjusted $p < 0.001$). The median fell from 0.853 to 0.819 and the share of files at or above 0.85 from 52.9\% to 34.5\%. The drop was largest in Fineract, where only 3 of 98 files (3.1\%) reached 0.85.

Replacing the exemplars (C2) raised cosine in 145 files and lowered it in 110 (median paired difference $+0.008$; Holm-adjusted $p < 0.001$). Under C2 the median is 0.875 and lies significantly above 0.85 (one-sided $p < 0.001$), so a different choice of exemplars would reverse the RQ1 decision. Descriptively, almost all of this change comes from Fineract (median 0.827 under C0, 0.876 under C2), while Sylius and Diaspora change by less than 0.01.

Parser compliance was similar under all three prompts. Under C0, C1 and C2, 252, 252 and 254 of the 255 files parse (exact McNemar against C0, Holm-adjusted $p = 1.0$ for both); the tests do not establish equivalence. Outputs were longer under C1 and C2 than under C0 (median 764 and 772 completion tokens against 509).

\subsection{Consolidated Answers}

RQ1: not supported. The median similarity is not demonstrably above 0.85, only Sylius exceeds it, and the conclusion changes with the threshold within a few hundredths and with the choice of exemplars. RQ2: yes. Nearly all generated files are valid Gherkin, with or without an example in the prompt. High similarity does not guarantee correct content: 25 of 60 audited high-scoring files contain a business-logic defect, and seeded defects leave the metric almost unchanged.
