\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluated GPT-5.4-mini for Gherkin generation on Connextra-style user stories reverse-engineered from 261 feature files of three open-source projects, with the three prompt exemplars excluded from the primary analysis ($N = 258$). The two research questions produce different answers. For RQ1, the median skeleton cosine similarity was 0.853 against a threshold of 0.85, and the one-sided Wilcoxon test gave $p = 0.474$ ($\delta = 0.054$; $p = 0.355$ with all 261 files). We fail to reject $H_0$ and do not claim that GPT-5.4-mini exceeds the threshold. Only the e-commerce project exceeded it, while the finance and social-network projects lay significantly below it, and the conclusion would change at a threshold of 0.84. For RQ2, 255 of 258 generated files were valid Gherkin (98.84\%, 95\% CI [96.64\%, 99.76\%], $p < 0.001$), so we reject $H_0$. This corrects the 100\% of the submitted version.

The revision analyses limit what the similarity results mean. In a sample of 60 files at or above the threshold, 25 contained a business-logic defect, and cosine did not separate defective from non-defective files significantly. Seeded defects left the score almost unchanged. These labels are AI-drafted and human-reviewed, and their agreement with an independent rater was below our protocol's threshold of 0.7 ($\kappa = 0.571$ and $0.609$). A prompt ablation shows that the RQ1 decision also depends on the exemplars: with a different randomly drawn exemplar per project the median rises to 0.875 and exceeds 0.85 significantly, mostly because of Fineract, and without an example it falls to 0.819. Parser compliance stayed high under all three prompts (252 to 254 of 255 files).

For practice, the supported use case is assisted drafting: a QA engineer can start from a valid feature file and focus review on expected outcomes, conditions, data values and criterion coverage. Neither a parser pass nor a high similarity score supports autonomous acceptance. Future work should (1) use independently written requirements, (2) validate semantics with criterion-level review by independent raters or with execution against step definitions, and (3) compare retrieval-augmented and open-source baselines on the same files.
