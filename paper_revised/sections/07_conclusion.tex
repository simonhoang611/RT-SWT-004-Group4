\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluated how well GPT-5.4-mini reconstructs 261 feature files of three open-source projects from Connextra-style user stories reverse-engineered from them, with the three prompt exemplars excluded from the primary analysis ($N = 258$). The main contribution is an auditable multi-domain evaluation that separates parser compliance from structural resemblance and demonstrates, through defect auditing and perturbation, why embedding similarity cannot certify business correctness. The two research questions produce different answers. For RQ1, the median skeleton cosine similarity was 0.853 against the reference point of 0.85, and the one-sided exact sign test gave $p = 0.209$ ($\delta = 0.054$; $p = 0.161$ with all 261 files; Wilcoxon $p = 0.474$). We fail to reject $H_0$ and do not claim that GPT-5.4-mini exceeds the reference point. Only the e-commerce project exceeded it, while the finance project lay significantly below it in both tests and the social-network project only in the Wilcoxon test, and the conclusion would change at a reference point of 0.84. For RQ2, 255 of 258 generated files were valid Gherkin (98.84\%, 95\% CI [96.64\%, 99.76\%], $p < 0.001$), so we reject $H_0$. This corrects the 100\% of the submitted version.

The revision analyses limit what the similarity results mean. In an exploratory audit of 60 files at or above the reference point, 25 contained a business-logic defect, and cosine did not separate defective from non-defective files significantly. Seeded defects left the score almost unchanged. These labels are AI-drafted and human-reviewed, and their agreement with an independent rater was below our protocol's threshold of 0.7 ($\kappa = 0.571$ and $0.609$); a consensus round on the 15 doubly labelled files raised the defective files among them from 5 to 8. In a same-time rerun, removing the example lowered the median from 0.852 to 0.811, more than repeated runs of the same prompt vary; a different randomly drawn exemplar per project raised it to 0.873, mostly because of Fineract, but its paired effect stayed within that run-to-run variation, which moved a fifth to a third of the repeated files across 0.85. Parser compliance stayed high under all three prompts (252 to 255 of 255 files).

For practice, and pending a test on independently written stories, the supported use case is assisted drafting: a QA engineer can start from a valid feature file and focus review on expected outcomes, conditions, data values and criterion coverage. The defect evidence behind this advice is exploratory, comes from a high-similarity sample stratified by project, and does not estimate the defect rate of the whole corpus. Neither a parser pass nor a high similarity score supports autonomous acceptance. Future work should (1) use independently written requirements, (2) validate semantics with criterion-level review by independent raters or with execution against step definitions, and (3) compare retrieval-augmented and open-source baselines on the same files.
