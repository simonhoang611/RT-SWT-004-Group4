\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study evaluated how well GPT-5.4-mini reconstructs 261 feature files of three open-source projects from Connextra-style user stories reverse-engineered from them ($N = 258$ without the three prompt exemplars). The main contribution is an auditable multi-domain evaluation that separates parser compliance from structural resemblance and demonstrates, through defect auditing and perturbation, why embedding similarity cannot certify business correctness. For RQ1, the median skeleton cosine similarity was 0.853 and the one-sided exact sign test gave $p = 0.209$ ($\delta = 0.054$; Wilcoxon $p = 0.474$), so we fail to reject $H_0$ and do not claim that GPT-5.4-mini exceeds the reference point of 0.85; only the e-commerce project exceeded it, and the conclusion would change at 0.84. For RQ2, 255 of 258 generated files were valid Gherkin (98.84\%, $p < 0.001$), so we reject $H_0$. In an exploratory audit, 25 of 60 files at or above the reference point contained a business-logic defect, and seeded defects left the score almost unchanged. These labels are AI-drafted and human-reviewed, their agreement with an independent rater was below our protocol's threshold of 0.7, and a consensus round raised the defective files among the 15 doubly labelled ones from 5 to 8. In a same-time rerun, removing the example lowered the median from 0.852 to 0.811, while a different exemplar had a paired effect within the run-to-run variation.

For practice, and pending a test on independently written stories, the supported use case is assisted drafting: a QA engineer can start from a valid feature file and focus review on expected outcomes, conditions, data values and criterion coverage. The defect evidence behind this advice is exploratory, comes from a high-similarity sample stratified by project, and does not estimate the defect rate of the whole corpus. Neither a parser pass nor a high similarity score supports autonomous acceptance. Future work should (1) use independently written requirements, (2) validate semantics with criterion-level review by independent raters or with execution against step definitions, and (3) compare retrieval-augmented and open-source baselines on the same files.
